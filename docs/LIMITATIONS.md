# Limitations and what the rehearsal does and does not prove

**What a pass proves:** the clarified spec is executable by a scripted SO-101 program across the stated band in rigid-body physics with a geometric grasp, and the success criteria are computable; not that a learned policy would succeed or a real grasp holds.

Three failing rollouts (an insertion collision, an unreachable target, and what the rehearsal can show of a wrong aperture), each with the simulator's reason and what the patching model said, are in [FAILURES.md](FAILURES.md).

## Honest limits

Every piece exists (MediaPipe; GPT-4V for Robotics, SeeDo, GF-VLA; MimicGen, DexMimicGen, GR00T-Mimic; IWSDS 2026, AmbResVLM; GR00T-Dreams, Nebius robot-sdg). New is the loop and its measurement: a cheap model plays the robot and its compile errors are the questionnaire; each answer patches the spec, with per-field provenance; the simulator rehearses the program on Earth and in orbit, and its failure notes go back to the model until a patch passes. On /try, once a program passes, the patching model writes twelve perturbations of the world from object symmetry and the tightest constraint (or the page builds them from the spec with no model), each run blind and sensed on Earth and in orbit, for a perception budget per axis: how far each mode got on each side before its first failure, only what was run. On the NASA samples blind often survives as wide a band as sensed, because the grasp is geometric: the grid shows where perception matters (the seed film in orbit: blind 5 of 12, sensed 10 of 12), not that it always does. On each of the five manipulation pages of the space showcase (not the wave, which has nothing to perturb), a button runs every recorded version of the program (first compile, after the answers, each Notes patch) on that clip's same twelve, blind and sensed on Earth and in orbit, all on the last version's spec, and shows passes out of 24 per gravity per version, as they score (a later version may score lower). The first bar is a baseline, retarget and replay: a program written by fixed rules from the label's steps as fitted to the bench, with no model and no answers, scored the same way; it is our rule set, one stand-in for "the standard approach", not a published method, and where its rules do not reach (a place the label gives nowhere to go, a gesture) the page says so instead of scoring it. On Earth it scores as well as the last version or better on three of the five clips (24 against 24, 22 against 21, 24 against 22 of 24); in orbit it falls behind on all five (3 against 15, 0 against 19, 8 against 13, and 0 on the pipette and the chess clip, where it stops at a move out of reach, against 22 and 22). On /try the same scoreboard runs under the grid, once the grid has run, on the page's own versions. It is not in the export. On /try, off by default: a playbook, six rules written by hand from the showcase's Notes patches that fixed a failure and from the bench's two distilled rules, each text with its evidence, tagged by the kind of task it was learned on and sent to the compiling model only for that kind; the program says which it applied, which is the model's own account. It is not learning across clips in any measured sense: on four NASA pocket clips, with the rules learned on the other clips the first program passed at 1 g and 0 g in 0 of 32 compiles, against 6 of 32 for the same request with its rules taken out and 8 of 32 with the switch off. Designed, not on the site yet: the answers as tolerance bands. On the site since 29 October: a reshoot list from Nemotron 3 Ultra, behind a button. The answers are the demonstrator's claims; the rehearsal checks whether a scripted program can honour them, not whether they are true. Limits: one 5-DoF arm plus gripper, geometric grasp, primitive objects, approximate calibration, a format-valid skeleton.

## Simulated versus real

- One 5-DoF arm plus a gripper (SO-101 from MuJoCo Menagerie); no humanoid, no dexterous hand.
- Liquid: a position-based particle fluid (`lib/sim/fluid.ts`), one-way coupled to MuJoCo: the cup's walls move the water, the water does not load the arm or the cup. At most 600 particles of 4 mm (38 mL) to keep 60 fps; a larger volume is cut, and the page says so. The settled volume reads about 5% low, and surface tension is not calibrated to water. What the arm shows: on Earth a 120° pour lands about 95% in the bowl; in orbit a slow pour leaves the water in the cup, and turning the cup over in one second lands 85 to 93% (90% or more in 1 of 6 seeds, against 3 of 6 for the hinge-mounted cup of the measurement, because the 5-DoF arm turns the cup about 8° aside). In orbit an open cup that is carried loses its water before any pour, so the demo cup starts at pour height.
- Hands: MediaPipe's hand model returns nothing on nitrile gloves and on hands under about thirty pixels. The sepia wrist crop recovers hands in many frames of the ISS clip, not all; where it does not, the pose model's wrist carries the motion and the hand aperture is an interview question, not a measurement.
- Grasp is a geometric weld gate: no friction, no slip, no deformation, and no aperture: the jaws close until both touch the object, whatever `aperture_mm` the program gives, and a box 120 x 120 mm across and 40 mm tall is held too ([FAILURES.md](FAILURES.md), case 3).
- Toss and catch (#159, in the simulator and the program schema, not yet in the compile prompt): a toss lets go while the gripper moves and MuJoCo carries the object as a rigid body, with no drag, so a scrap of paper flies like a block; the timing is scripted (a run-up, a release, a brake) and the catch predicts the path from the object's own state as it leaves the pads, with nothing watching it in flight. A catch is the same geometric grasp, counted only when the jaws stop on the object as they held it. Measured with hand-written programs over ten seeds (docs/MEASUREMENTS.md section 18): thrown straight up and caught in the same gripper on Earth 8 of 10 (the two misses are the grasp before the throw), but 2 of 10 with the brake asking the joints for a little more; pushed across and caught by the same arm in orbit 0 of 10. It is not a reliable catch, and the site does not offer it: the compile route refuses an answer that uses these ops, as it refuses any op it does not know. A toss asks the joints for up to 4 rad/s (the modelled servos reach about 4.8 unloaded), twice the 2 rad/s a gesture is held to: the gesture cap keeps a traced path within a millimetre, and a toss needs the speed to throw at all.
- Gestures (a wave, a signal): the path is the pose model's estimate of the centre of one hand (its wrist, index and pinky landmarks) in the person's frontal plane (depth is dropped), scaled by the ratio of the arm lengths into a plane in front of the robot and centred there. The only check is that the point between the pads passes within the stated distance of each point of the path, in order and at most a quarter of a second after the point's own time; a gesture has no task success. When a joint would have to turn faster than 2 rad/s the arm follows slower than the clip, and the page says by how much; that cap was chosen from the simulation, not from a real servo. A gesture's similar tasks are built from its path with no model: mirrored to the arm's other side, slower (one and a half times slower than the arm can follow the clip at all, estimated from the hand's speed against the 170 mm/s or more the arm kept up with in the rehearsal, since the arm already follows a quick path slower than the clip and a path slowed less would change nothing it does; the joints set the cap, not the hand, so the card says when the arm was still held back), and half again as big, or 0.6 times as big when the path already fills the box in front of the arm. They are the same path transformed, not new gestures, and a line typed for a gesture is not read.
- The demo scene stands in for the NASA seed film clip with primitives: the film is a stiff 15 x 6 x 40 mm strip (a real film is far thinner and flexible, too thin for the gripper) and the plant pillow a rigid box with a pocket 5 mm wider than the strip.
- Objects are primitives sized from a monocular clip; sizes are approximate and are the first thing the interview checks.
- The exported datasets (the last step of the 1-minute demo, and section 8 of Try it once the program passes on Earth and in orbit) are format-valid LeRobot v2.1 skeletons in the layout GR00T's loader reads: joint states and actions of the scripted program's passing rollouts, not checked with GR00T's loader. The demo's holds no video. Try it's holds, by default, one MP4 per episode drawn again in the browser from the simulator state the stage kept (the bare bench, the stage's default camera, the arm's meshes and the objects as primitives): a picture of the simulation, never footage. Try it's also carries the session (spec, program, answers, versions, the page's model calls, `grounding.json` when the web lookup ran, the clip's source and licence). They are not training data and the quality report says so, file by file.
- A pass does not predict that a learned policy or a real robot would succeed.
- The station module and the kitchen counter drawn around the arm once a program passes are decoration: three.js scenery with no part in the physics. The rehearsal always runs on the bench, and the setting says so on the stage.

## Orbit mode

Orbit mode is rigid-body physics with gravity set to 0 g (the Moon preset uses 0.1654 g). It models one thing well: a released object keeps its velocity instead of resting, so a program must constrain an object before it lets go. It does not model air currents, tethers and cable drag, contact with floating neighbours, the arm's own reaction forces on a free-flying base, flight rules, or any certification. A drifting object in the rehearsal is a model of the failure, not a flight test.

## Live budget and what is kept

- Live Token Factory calls stop at a total of US$250 at list prices (`UNDERSTUDY_BUDGET_USD`), counted in one store across every instance. Each call reserves the most it can cost before it is made (its whole answer budget, its prompt over-counted) and settles to its list price after, so calls running at once cannot pass the cap; prices are Token Factory's list prices from `tests/fixtures/models.json`, not the invoice, and a model missing from that list is counted at the dearest price in it. After that, and whenever the store cannot be read, every step replays its recording and says why. The cap counts Token Factory only, not Tavily.
- For a clip of your own, and only once you tick the consent box on `/try` ("Clips you submit, with what the models made of them, may be kept and used to improve Understudy and to train models."), the site keeps the session: the small copy of the clip the model watches (480 pixels wide, 4 frames a second, no sound), the line you typed, the label, the task spec, your answers, every program, each rehearsal's result and each patch, under a random session id, with no name, account or address, and never the unlock code. It sits in a private Upstash for Redis store that only the team reads, to see where the models go wrong and to build the training-data log of [#78](https://github.com/gibouu/understudy/issues/78) for fine-tuning. Without the box ticked nothing is kept, the samples are never kept, and unticking the box stops keeping the clip on screen. The page shows the session id; quote it in an issue to have the session deleted.
