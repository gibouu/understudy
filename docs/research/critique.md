## Critic attack:robotics-skeptic: FAIL
- [critical] The perturbation grid cannot falsify anything as designed: program targets are object-relative and the program is handed the perturbed object pose, and the grasp is a weld-on-contact. Moving the tube moves the targets, so every scenario inside reach passes regardless of the tolerance band the demonstrator stated. The headline promise ('does the task survive a tube 4 cm to the left') and the self-scored 9 for 'perturbations test the spec' rest on a loop that only tests reachability and obstacle clearance.
  evidence: §7: 'The executed motion is the Nano program, whose targets are object- and zone-relative (move_to("object:tube", ...)), so moving the tube moves the targets, MimicGen-style' plus 'Grasp uses a weld-on-contact rule'. §6 Saboteur: '4 inside, 4 at the edge, 2 beyond (expect fail)'. §2: 'finds out whether a task as demonstrated survives a tube that is 4 cm to the left'. §16: 'makes perturbations test the spec rather than gripper width'. MimicGen itself assumes a known object pose at generation time; its filter is success under that assumption, not robustness to pose error.
  fix: Run every scenario in two execution modes and report both: 'sensed' (program receives the perturbed pose; tests reach, clearance, predicate computability) and 'blind' (program receives the nominal pose from the spec; the world is perturbed; tests how much pose error the scripted program tolerates). The interview tolerance band is evaluated in blind mode; 'inside/edge/beyond' only means something there. Reframe the headline honestly and still impressively: 'Understudy measures how much perception this task needs: blind, the program fails beyond 12 mm; with a pose estimate, it survives the whole tray.' Add a third optional axis, pose-estimate noise (Gaussian mm), which is the real-world quantity a QA desk cares about.
- [major] The SO-101 is a 5-DoF arm plus a gripper (shoulder_pan, shoulder_lift, elbow_flex, wrist_flex, wrist_roll, gripper). The program's move_to(frame, offset, orient) with a full orientation from the hand-plane normal is unreachable for most human hand poses; damped-least-squares IK on six constraints will stall at a residual and time out. Those timeouts will be reported as task failures and fed to Ultra's reshoot list as spec or footage gaps.
  evidence: §7: 'the hand-plane normal gives orientation', 'resolves each target to joint angles with a 60-line damped-least-squares IK', 'steps until converged or timed out'. Menagerie robotstudio_so101 is listed as 6 DoF including the gripper; GR00T's cube_to_bowl_5 state is shoulder_pan.pos..gripper.pos (5 arm joints + gripper).
  fix: Solve IK for position plus a single orientation DoF (gripper roll about the approach axis); project the requested approach direction onto the arm's reachable wrist-pitch plane and record the projection angle. Run a reachability pre-check per scenario (position inside envelope, projection angle under a threshold) before stepping physics, and classify failures from that check as 'embodiment limit' with the number attached. State in README and on the stage that the SO-101 cannot reproduce arbitrary human hand orientations and that top-down versus side approaches are chosen by the arm, not the human.
- [major] The human trajectory is never retargeted or executed; the robot runs a synthesised primitive program while the UI shows 'the SO-101 joints the retargeted path will drive' and a ghost trail over the arm. Judges will see an arm that does not follow the hand and ask why; a reviewer will note the video contributes only segment order, grasp type and aperture, and most of its information is discarded.
  evidence: §5 Row 3: 'six gauges for the SO-101 joints the retargeted path will drive'; §5: 'draws the human comet trail as a ghost over the robot, so retargeting is visible without narration'; §7: 'The executed motion is the Nano program'. One-liner: 'compiles that spec into a robot program'.
  fix: Keep the program as the skeleton but source its waypoints from the clip: per VL segment, take 3 to 5 wrist via-points (pre-grasp approach, grasp, lift apex, carry midpoint, place) expressed in the anchored object or zone frame, timing-scaled, each tagged source: pose#segN. The interpreter moves through them; a perturbation transforms them per segment, which is the actual MimicGen mechanism. The ghost trail then matches the arm's path within the reach projection, the Row 3 gauges become honest, and the README says 'anchored via-points', not 'retargeted trajectory'. Cost: one function in lib/sim/interpreter.ts and one field on steps.
- [major] The weld-on-contact grasp makes most predicates trivial and the planned fail-patch-pass demo hard to produce naturally. Once welded, contact_both_pads, height_above and tilt_under are properties of the gripper, not the object; the 'outcome without agency' rule guards against a teleport the simulator cannot generate; and the scripted 'no sustained contact' failure at 1:50-2:25 only arises when the fingers never touch, i.e. an IK or anchoring miss.
  evidence: §7: 'an equality constraint activates when both finger geoms touch the object while the gripper is closing'; predicates contact_both_pads, height_above, tilt_under; 'an object that ends in the zone without a sustained gripper contact beforehand is a fail'. §14: 'fail, "no sustained contact". Notes card: Super VL proposes a wider pre-grasp approach; apply; re-run; pass'.
  fix: Gate the weld on geometry instead of mere touch: both pads must close within ±x mm of the object's centre along the pad normal and the commanded aperture must land inside [size-δ, size+δ]; otherwise the object stays a free body and falls. Measure tilt and zone membership on the object after release, not during the weld. Describe grasp success in the README as 'a geometric proxy for a pinch grasp; no friction, no slip'. Move the demo failure to the insertion at the rack (20 mm slot versus 17 mm tube with real collision geometry): a position error of a few mm produces an honest collision failure, and the Notes patch ('raise the pre-insert waypoint, descend vertically') is a believable fix.
- [major] The perturbation axes are insensitive for the hero task. Yaw on a cylindrical tube changes nothing; ±10 percent scale on a 17 mm tube is 1.7 mm, invisible to a weld grasp; object x/y is absorbed by object-relative targets. The grid will be uniformly green inside tolerance and the sliders will not react in any way a judge can see.
  evidence: §3: 'tolerances: object_offset_mm 40, object_yaw_deg 90, scale [0.9,1.1], obstacle allowed'; §7: 'three sliders (object x/y, scale, obstacle position)'.
  fix: Let Super choose axes from object symmetry and the task's tight constraint, and bias toward what a scripted program must get right: rack or zone pose error (±mm), slot clearance (modelled, not a box), tube tilt at pick, start height, and an obstacle on the carry path; drop yaw for symmetric objects and declare it in the scenario rationale.
