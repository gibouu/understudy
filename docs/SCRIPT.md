# Demo video script (three minutes or less)

Recorded on the live site (`/try` and `/space`) with each section's model name visible, and the prices where the page shows them (the Notes patches, the similar tasks, the showcase). Plain-text captions naming each model ID as it is used. No logos, no music, no third-party trademarks. Every shot is something the site does today; the numbers that come from the bench harness, not from the site, are said to be measurements. Five moments a judge should lean in for: the model getting the clip wrong and right after one line, a question changing the program, the robot rehearsing on the stage, the fail in orbit turning into a pass after a patch, and the price of the whole session.

- **0:00-0:12 Cold open.** A bottle tossed from one hand to the other, slowed down; the model's first label is wrong. VO: "A robot has no idea what it just saw. And at first, neither did our AI."
- **0:12-0:30 The mission.** NASA public-domain footage of an astronaut slipping seed film into a plant pillow (`samples/iss-veggie-seed-film.mp4`). Caption: "One hour of ISS crew time: $130,000 (NASA)". VO: an astronaut's hour, every routine task a robot takes over gives one back, and teaching a robot is film, fail, film again.
- **0:30-0:52 What the model saw.** Upload on Try it, "What the model saw", one line typed, the clip read again and right. Caption `nvidia/Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL` on Nebius Token Factory. The one-line correction is the confirm step of issue #158 (PR #163): film it on the deployed site once that is merged, on a local build until then.
- **0:52-1:10 Questions from the robot.** The ISS seed film on Try it: the program with its question slots, a question answered (the seed film's is a choice of success check), the program recompiling. Caption: the compiling model named on the section. VO: "Every number it would have to guess becomes a question for the person who did the task."
- **1:10-2:10 Rehearse: a full minute of the arm working.** The SO-101 in MuJoCo, in the browser, on Earth (1 g): a pass. "Try a similar task": the model's twists on the same task, each rehearsed on Earth and in orbit. The switch to orbit (0 g): the released object drifts, fail. The Notes loop: the failure note as text, a first patch that still fails, a second that passes, each with its tokens and price. VO on the number: "On our test programs, this loop fixed ninety-four percent of the failures it could fix" (bench harness, `docs/MEASUREMENTS.md` section 8).
- **2:10-2:30 The price.** The space showcase: "How it improved", one row per version of the program with its price, and the session total. VO: "seven calls to Nemotron on Nebius Token Factory, under half a cent" (the seed film recording: 7 calls, $0.0040).
- **2:30-2:52 Next.** A clip about a person (lip balm): what the model saw after one line, and a one-arm idea. VO: next, any footage, turned into robot training for places we have not been yet. Said as next, with no date.
- **2:52-3:00 End card.** "Understudy. Show it once." Demo URL, repository, "Nemotron on Nebius Token Factory. Simulated arm, rigid-body physics: not hardware, not a flight test."

Not on the site, so not in the video: the twelve-scenario saboteur grid, blind versus sensed runs and the perception budget, the scoreboard on 24 rollouts, the playbook carried to the next clip and the Moon on Try it, and Ultra's reshoot list. The README lists them under "Next". The export zip came to the site with the 1-minute demo after the video was cut (its last step downloads a LeRobot skeleton of the rollouts it played), so the video does not show it. The playbook take of the first voiceover ("what it learned on Earth and on Mars… five percent to thirty-three") is a bench measurement with no panel on the site: say it as a measurement over a caption card citing `docs/MEASUREMENTS.md` section 8, or cut it.

## Physical AI requirement

At least one minute of the video shows the key application modules in action: the rehearsal stage, the similar tasks rehearsed on Earth and in orbit, and the fail, patch, pass loop. The app has no hardware component; the README says so.
